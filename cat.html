Cats

The Journey of Cats

Cats were first domesticated around 7500 BCE in Western Asia and are the second most popular domestic pet in the U.S. While there are over 60 unique breeds, some general care practices apply to all cats regardless of breed. In this section, you'll find tips on caring for your cat at various stages of life.

From Birth to One Year

In the first weeks, kittens should nurse directly from their mother, also known as the queen. If any kittens struggle to grow, it might be necessary for the caretaker to bottle-feed or use a feeding tube. Kittens typically start transitioning to solid food by three to four weeks. Wet kitten food is ideal during this period for its extra nutrients.

One to Seven Years: Cat Stage

As cats mature, their dietary needs change. Limit their food intake to avoid obesity, offering two meals a day instead of leaving food out constantly. This helps maintain a healthy weight and prevents common health issues.

Seven Years and Beyond: Senior Cats

As cats age, they need fewer calories and more high-quality protein. Check labels carefully to ensure the food contains named protein sources (e.g., salmon) rather than general categories like "fish," which often indicates lower-quality ingredients.
